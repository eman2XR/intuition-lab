### Scenes and the player
- Scene fields from the writer: title (≤100), subtitle (≤250, the home card teaser), why (≤220, about 25 words, the line under the title), three or four beats (title ≤90, body ≤380), `mass` 0–1 and `distance` 1–2.5 for the two sliders, `controls` (none, first, second, both), `duration` (it can only stretch the timing), and the `takeaway` shown at the end. Hand-made scenes may have more chapters and may add `links` (the first mention in the why line becomes a link), `opening` and `next`, which the writer's schema does not offer.
- Subtitles play one sentence at a time at 14 characters a second, at least 2.8 s each, with pauses; decimals never split a sentence. The render checks require at most 15 characters a second, at most three sentences a chapter, at least 2.5 s on screen and at most 80 characters a subtitle (`SUBTITLE_CHARACTERS`). Since 2026-11-14 (owner) subtitles read like film subtitles: one short spoken idea each, about 12 words, the first line setting a concrete scene ("Imagine ..."); add a chapter rather than pack one. A subtitle longer than one line breaks after the comma nearest its middle when both halves fit (`phrased` in `web/scene.js`).
- Chapter 1 sets the first control to zero. The last chapter's demo moves both controls unless the engine scripts its own (`demo(p, spec)`) or sets `demo: false`. Sliders appear inside the player only after playback or from the sliders button; nothing moves before play. Only the guitar-string engine makes sound (a plucked string); its mute setting is remembered in `council-sound`.
- `ENGINES` has 25 engines. Agents can use the 17 in `TOPICS`; the engines of hand-made guides (river, fall-views, tides, lift, moon-tides, birthdays, sky-colour, simultaneity) have no agent notes. `simultaneity` (Einstein's train) draws the platform view (strikes simultaneous, the moving train shortened by gamma, each replay holding once its flashes arrive) and the train view (the platform and clouds slide back, the front struck first by `Physics.simulGap` = v L); chapter 1 is at rest, the last chapter holds the result and the readout says "Both ends struck together" at zero speed. ### Adding a picture engine and marking it ready
1. `web/scene.js`: a draw function and an `ENGINES` entry (slider labels `a` and `b`, formatters `fa` and `fb`, `measure` or none, `disclosure`, `try`, `aria`, `demo` or `demoEnd`, and `effect(a, b)` when the readout is not zero at zero). Draw labels with `text()` and `pill()` so they are counted; pass `content: true` only for the words of a sentence being studied. Without a chapter clock (thumbnails), draw a representative still.
2. `web/physics.js`: the exact model and a checks set registered in `checkSets()`, so `checksFor` and CI's `allChecks()` include it.
3. `lab/topics.py`: notes that end with `COMMON` (what each chapter shows, the honesty points, suggested sources) and a `TOPICS` entry. `web/app.js`: a category in `TOPICS` (and in `REVIEWED` when the accuracy review is not physics). `lab/fixtures.py`: an `ENGINE_SCENES` entry keyed by a phrase only its notes contain.
4. Render it with `python3 -m lab run --provider fixture --topic <kind> --rounds 2`; every check must pass, the label checks included. Then run a frames-only blind-viewer subagent and a skeptical-learner subagent (frames plus narration, transfer questions answered from the guide alone, "not covered" allowed), fix, re-test and copy-edit. These are proxies; the owner answering cold is the real test.
5. Mark it ready only after that: add it to `READY` and set `ready: true` on its item in `web/curriculum.json`. A draft or a published guide using the model keeps it from being planned, so an engine meant for the daily run is marked ready rather than drafted.

Automated, gated engine builds (`lab/build_engine.py`) follow the same shape: the model returns `ENGINE_SCHEMA` fields (`physics`, `scene`, `entry`, `checksName`, `kind`, `label`, `category`, `model_notes`), which the installer splices at the `/* AGENT-ENGINES: ... */` markers (scene, map, physics, checkSets, app-topics) and the `# AGENT-ENGINES: entries` marker in `lab/topics.py` (where it appends a `<KIND>_NOTES = '''...''' + COMMON`, a `TOPICS[...]` entry and `READY.add(...)`), and sets `ready: true` on the curriculum item. Marks: adding markers moves line numbers, so grep for them rather than hard-coding lines. The gates are `node --check` on `scene.js`/`physics.js`/`app.js`; `allChecks()` with zero failures plus at least three non-vacuous checks for the new kind that reference the model functions; a function-only structural rule on the `scene` field (`top_level_declarations_only`: only function and named `const`/`let` declarations, so nothing runs at load — no IIFEs or bare calls); the locked-down `gate_probe` sandbox render (blocks all network and throws on timers, workers, sockets, beacons and `document.cookie`, so side-effecting code fails behaviourally, not by regex); and `lab.render.capture` of `provisional_scene` with every render check passing. `validate_engine` also keeps a denylist (dynamic code, randomness, a clock) as defence in depth. Any failure, at any step, restores the snapshotted bytes, removes any draft, deletes any half-made branch, and leaves nothing staged on `main`. On success the engine code and draft go onto an `engine-candidate/<day>-<kind>` branch (pushed on its own, never `main`), the `main` working tree is restored byte-clean, and the owner gets a proposal (kind `engine`) and a dashboard note. The result is never a `READY`-marked published engine on `main`: the owner reviews the branch, merges it with `approve_engine`/`lab approve-engine <branch>` (ff-only after the gates re-run), then promotes the guide by hand with the human steps above (fixtures `ENGINE_SCENES` entry, blind-viewer and skeptic subagents, copy-edit) before it is published.
