The owner is learning physics and values visual intuition over equations; since 2026-10-29 the lab also covers mathematics and famous ideas (attention, shared birthdays). ### Scenes and the player
- Scene fields from the writer: title (≤100), subtitle (≤250, the home card teaser), why (≤220, about 25 words, the line under the title), three or four beats (title ≤90, body ≤380), `mass` 0–1 and `distance` 1–2.5 for the two sliders, `controls` (none, first, second, both), `duration` (it can only stretch the timing), and the `takeaway` shown at the end. Hand-made scenes may have more chapters and may add `links` (the first mention in the why line becomes a link), `opening` and `next`, which the writer's schema does not offer.
- Subtitles play one sentence at a time at 14 characters a second, at least 2.8 s each, with pauses; decimals never split a sentence. The render checks require at most 15 characters a second, at most three sentences a chapter, at least 2.5 s on screen and at most 80 characters a subtitle (`SUBTITLE_CHARACTERS`). Since 2026-11-14 (owner) subtitles read like film subtitles: one short spoken idea each, about 12 words, the first line setting a concrete scene; since 2026-11-23 (owner) the way in varies from guide to guide (a plain scene, something the viewer has noticed, a question, something to try), and "Imagine" is used at most once a guide, not as every opener; add a chapter rather than pack one. A subtitle longer than one line breaks after the comma nearest its middle when both halves fit (`phrased` in `web/scene.js`).
- Chapter 1 sets the first control to zero. The last chapter's demo moves both controls unless the engine scripts its own (`demo(p, spec)`) or sets `demo: false`. Sliders appear inside the player only after playback or from the sliders button; nothing moves before play. Only the guitar-string engine makes sound (a plucked string); its mute setting is remembered in `council-sound`.
- `ENGINES` has 25 engines. Agents can use the 17 in `TOPICS`; the engines of hand-made guides (river, fall-views, tides, lift, moon-tides, birthdays, sky-colour, simultaneity) have no agent notes. `simultaneity` (Einstein's train) draws the platform view (strikes simultaneous, the moving train shortened by gamma, each replay holding once its flashes arrive) and the train view (the platform and clouds slide back, the front struck first by `Physics.simulGap` = v L); chapter 1 is at rest, the last chapter holds the result and the readout says "Both ends struck together" at zero speed. Out of `READY`: standing-waves, quantum-well, wave-packet and attention have fixed choreography that agent-written scenes would not match; twin-trip shows what rather than why; spacetime, falling-time and tunneling are graph-style; two-slit-particles failed a beginner test.

### Adding a picture engine and marking it ready
1. `web/scene.js`: a draw function and an `ENGINES` entry (slider labels `a` and `b`, formatters `fa` and `fb`, `measure` or none, `disclosure`, `try`, `aria`, `demo` or `demoEnd`, and `effect(a, b)` when the readout is not zero at zero). Draw labels with `text()` and `pill()` so they are counted; pass `content: true` only for the words of a sentence being studied. Without a chapter clock (thumbnails), draw a representative still.
2. `web/physics.js`: the exact model and a checks set registered in `checkSets()`, so `checksFor` and CI's `allChecks()` include it.
3. `lab/topics.py`: notes that end with `COMMON` (what each chapter shows, the honesty points, suggested sources) and a `TOPICS` entry. `web/app.js`: a category in `TOPICS` (and in `REVIEWED` when the accuracy review is not physics). `lab/fixtures.py`: an `ENGINE_SCENES` entry keyed by a phrase only its notes contain.
4. Render it with `python3 -m lab run --provider fixture --topic <kind> --rounds 2`; every check must pass, the label checks included. Then run a frames-only blind-viewer subagent and a skeptical-learner subagent (frames plus narration, transfer questions answered from the guide alone, "not covered" allowed), fix, re-test and copy-edit. These are proxies; the owner answering cold is the real test.
5. Mark it ready only after that: add it to `READY` and set `ready: true` on its item in `web/curriculum.json`. A draft or a published guide using the model keeps it from being planned, so an engine meant for the daily run is marked ready rather than drafted.

Automated, gated engine builds (`lab/build_engine.py`) follow the same shape: the model returns `ENGINE_SCHEMA` fields (`physics`, `scene`, `entry`, `checksName`, `kind`, `label`, `category`, `model_notes`), which the installer splices at the `/* AGENT-ENGINES: ... */` markers (scene, map, physics, checkSets, app-topics) and the `# AGENT-ENGINES: entries` marker in `lab/topics.py` (where it appends a `<KIND>_NOTES = '''...''' + COMMON`, a `TOPICS[...]` entry and `READY.add(...)`), and sets `ready: true` on the curriculum item. Marks: adding markers moves line numbers, so grep for them rather than hard-coding lines. The gates are `node --check` on `scene.js`/`physics.js`/`app.js`; `allChecks()` with zero failures plus at least three non-vacuous checks for the new kind that reference the model functions; a function-only structural rule on the `scene` field (`top_level_declarations_only`: only function and named `const`/`let` declarations, so nothing runs at load — no IIFEs or bare calls); the locked-down `gate_probe` sandbox render (blocks all network and throws on timers, workers, sockets, beacons and `document.cookie`, so side-effecting code fails behaviourally, not by regex); and `lab.render.capture` of `provisional_scene` with every render check passing. `validate_engine` also keeps a denylist (dynamic code, randomness, a clock) as defence in depth. Any failure, at any step, restores the snapshotted bytes, removes any draft, deletes any half-made branch, and leaves nothing staged on `main`. On success the engine code and draft go onto an `engine-candidate/<day>-<kind>` branch (pushed on its own, never `main`), the `main` working tree is restored byte-clean, and the owner gets a proposal (kind `engine`) and a dashboard note. The result is never a `READY`-marked published engine on `main`: the owner reviews the branch, merges it with `approve_engine`/`lab approve-engine <branch>` (ff-only after the gates re-run), then promotes the guide by hand with the human steps above (fixtures `ENGINE_SCENES` entry, blind-viewer and skeptic subagents, copy-edit) before it is published.

A failing `gh` is retried once after 15 s with its error line logged; after that the run plans without requests. On 10-31 `gh` failed while GitHub was unreachable; on 11-01 it read cleanly.
- `council:choose` (Pick for tomorrow, body `curriculum: <id>`): the oldest pick of a ready, unexplored item becomes the plan without a planner call. `council:question` (Ask a question) and `council:feedback` (Give feedback) go to the planner; on an idle morning a `council:feedback` issue whose body names a published guide (`guide: <id>`) is instead handled by the revision builder (`lab/revise.py`). `council:guidance` (Guide the lab, then Send to the 9 AM run): up to five notes go to the planner, designer, writer and reviewers, never above the quality bar, and the dashboard counts them. `council:approve` and `council:decline` (`proposal: P-…`) record owner decisions on proposals. `council:archive` and `council:unarchive` (body `guide: <id>`, from the "Archive this guide"/"Restore this guide" owner link in a guide's behind-the-scenes footer) hide a published guide from the home page or list it again; `apply_archive` runs them first thing in the cycle (before planning, even when paused), replies and closes the issue, and leaves a transient publish failure open to retry.
- The run comments on the request it answered and on each guidance note. Picks, questions and guidance notes close once an attempt reached review and stay open after an early stop; feedback closes once the planner has read it, or once a feedback revision ships for the guide it names (left open with a hand-revision note if the revision cannot run or rolls back; `.council-state/feedback-handled.json` stops a later run re-handling it). A reply says "published" only after the push succeeded.
- Proposals: the project review runs once per local day after a publishing cycle (not when paused), one research call and one structured call, and merges at most four proposals into `web/proposals.json` with owner decisions applied. When nothing is plannable, a publishing morning first tries to REVISE a published guide from owner feedback (`lab/revise.py`; gated, a `feedback-revision/<day>-<id>` review-branch candidate — see the engine section and the History entry): the oldest unhandled `council:feedback` issue whose body names a published guide (`guide: <id>`) becomes an in-place revision of that engine and its guide record, closed with a note when the candidate ships (or left open with a hand-revision note on a rollback), tracked in `.council-state/feedback-handled.json`. Only if there is no feedback to revise does it try to BUILD the engine the next waiting question needs (`lab/build_engine.py`; gated, a review candidate on a branch), and only if that cannot run or fully rolls back does it run `research_brief`: it researches the next waiting question (owner `council:question` issues first, then the next curriculum item with no ready engine, skipping ones already briefed or published) through the same researcher, scientist and designer steps and saves a ready-to-build brief to `web/briefs/<id>.json` (the model, its checks and a chapter plan), once a day, surfaced as a `blueprint` proposal; if there is nothing to research or it fails, it falls back to a design-only sketch for the next waiting question, once a day (`kind: blueprint`, id `P-<day>-9`). The revision, the build, the brief and the sketch all share one once-a-day idle slot. Approved blueprint proposals still wait for a supervised coding session; the engine builder and the feedback-revision builder are the automated, gated paths, and their output is a review-branch candidate the owner merges, never a guide pushed to `main` or the live site.

### Worker setup and session limits
- Runs use this checkout with the Framework Python 3.13 and Playwright, personal `gh` credentials and signing off locally. `python3 -m lab schedule` can still generate a launch agent, but launchd cannot run a project on the Desktop (macOS privacy protection), so a scheduled job would need a checkout elsewhere again.
- `daily --count N` caps attempts per local day; crashes and failed planning use a slot. Never delete ledger entries to get around it.
- The coding session's sandbox cannot run the Claude CLI (it exits with 71), reach the web, open ports, launch Chrome or submit launchd jobs (do not retry that); the GitHub CLI works. Playwright works through request routing, so renders, `lab.sitecheck`, `lab.preview` and `lab.qa` run in the session with the Framework Python. Research works from the worker, not from a session. Live runs start from the owner's Terminal or the 9 AM worker.
- The permission check can block pushes to `main`, commands in the worker or writes to `web/published/`. Do not route around it: commit locally and give the owner the exact commands (without a leading `!`).
